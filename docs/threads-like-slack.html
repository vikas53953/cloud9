The short answer

You were right, and what shipped went the wrong way

You asked to drag the thread's edge and make it big, and have it stay. That is exactly what Slack advertises and exactly what Buzz does on this machine.

You can now resize the secondary view panel and thread pane with arrow keys.

Genuinely undocumented — nobody should put a number here

An exhaustive list that leaves threads out, next to an article that puts threads in, is strong evidence of no. 3 · What Buzz actually does — I opened it and used it

Buzz 0.5.5, the copy installed on this machine at %LOCALAPPDATA%\Buzz\buzz-desktop.exe. It was opened, driven into #coding, and the divider dragged with real mouse events — and this time the sizes were read off the elements themselves over a debug port, not measured off a screenshot. That is why every number below is 15 px or so bigger than the version you read: the old method kept losing the width of the divider.

Yes — the divider is draggable, and Buzz says so out loud

Put the pointer on the divider and Buzz shows its own tooltip. This is the single most useful thing found all morning — and the detail worth copying is not the words but when it says them:

Measured on your maximised window, whose usable viewport is 1920 px. The earlier version of this page said 1936 — that is the outer window frame, not the area the app can actually draw in, and using it threw the arithmetic out.

The rule Buzz follows, and the number matters: the two sides are symmetric at about 300 px each — thread floor 308, channel floor 301. Neither side has a ceiling. You decide which one is big. There is no bigger floor on the room; the earlier version of this page invented one.

Does it stay where you put it?

Test I ranResultMeasured
Set the thread wide, switch to #general, come back, reopen the thread Stays — same width, to the pixelunchanged
Double-click the divider Resets to the default, exactly as the tooltip promises → 388 px

So Buzz remembers your width for as long as the app is open, but loses it when you quit. I think that part is a weakness, not a feature, and I would rather Cloud9 remembered it properly — see my proposal below.

Buzz has a second mode: make the thread take over — and you have asked for this one

One detail that is easy to miss and is worth copying: the expanded mode survives a restart even though the width does not. Buzz remembers which mode you were in but forgets how wide you dragged it. Cloud9 will remember both.

And on a narrow window it stops splitting altogether — same idea, different trigger

Look at those two pictures together. Buzz's take-over mode and Buzz's narrow-window behaviour are the same thing — the thread gets the whole area and there is one obvious way back. One you ask for, the other the window forces. That matters a great deal for what Cloud9 should do, and it is the heart of the fix in section 6.

It matters because Cloud9's window cannot go below 800 pixels, so that corner needs a real answer rather than a shrug.

4 · What Cloud9 does today

Underneath, the width is decided by one line that leaves you no say in it (styles.css:423):

clamp(232px, (viewport − 78 − 250) × 0.22, 280px)

  • At your 1920 px viewport that sum comes out at 280 px, the ceiling — not the 268 px this page said last time. 1592 × 0.22 is 350, which the ceiling cuts back to 280. 268 px only happens on a window of about 1546 px. The old figure was the right number for the wrong screen, and it contradicted the formula printed four paragraphs below it.
  • The 280 px at the end is a hard ceiling. Making your window bigger cannot push past it — which is why the last two attempts at "tuning the width" moved it by about 4 pixels and looked identical to you.
  • The whole app was searched for anything that responds to dragging — pointer handlers, pointer capture, splitters, resizers — across apps/desktop/src and apps/relay/src. There is nothing. The only near-misses are an unrelated size-watcher and the @-mention list. The panel was never draggable; there was never a handle to find. This has now been checked twice, independently.

The proposal

6 · What I want to build — please read this bit closely

You marked this section 👍 on the earlier version. The plan below is the same plan with four things corrected and two things added — the take-over mode you asked for, and keyboard resizing, which Slack ships and I had missed. Nothing that you approved has been quietly removed.

1. A divider you can grab

A grab strip on the thread panel's left edge. The pointer changes to the left-right arrows when you are over it, and it lights up so you can see it is a handle. You drag; the thread follows your pointer live.

2. It opens bigger than it does now, before you touch anything

Default 388 px instead of today's 280 px — the same as Buzz's measured default. So even if you never drag it, it is noticeably better the first time you open it.

3. The limits — corrected to what Buzz really does

The earlier version said the room could never go below 380 px. That number was invented. The shape was Buzz's, the figure was not. Measured properly, Buzz is symmetric at about 300 px on both sides. A 380 px room floor would have quietly cost you 80 px of maximum thread width for no reason anyone could state — which is the softest possible re-run of "we capped your thread". So:

  • The thread never goes below 300 px, matching Buzz's 308.
  • The room never goes below 300 px, matching Buzz's 301. That is the only limit, and it is a floor on the room, not a cap on the thread.
  • On your maximised screen the app has 1592 px to divide (1920 viewport, less the 78 px rail and the 250 px sidebar), so you can drag the thread anywhere from 300 px up to about 1292 px — less the few pixels the grab strip itself occupies, which is not chosen yet. Today's fixed 280 px becomes a range over four times as wide at the top.

The earlier version said "300 to about 1230 px". That was wrong twice over: 1230 was not even right for the 380 floor it was based on (1592 − 380 = 1212), and it was computed from 1936, the outer window frame, rather than the 1920 px the app can draw in.

One more correction, because the reason given was false even though the number was fine. The 300 px thread floor was justified by saying your composer's button row wraps at about 260 px and the floor exists to stop that. It is not true: styles.css:1948 tells that row to wrap on purpose, everywhere, and the comment beside it says why — a row that cannot wrap is a row that eventually scrolls sideways — and styles.css:1703 says it again for the thread's own composer. The wrapping is a deliberate feature, not a defect a floor has to prevent. The real reason for 300 is simply that it is what Buzz uses and it reads well.

4. You can resize it from the keyboard too — this is new

Slack's accessibility changelog, June 2026: You can now resize the secondary view panel and thread pane with arrow keys. My earlier plan had no keyboard way to resize at all, which would have shipped a control you can only reach with a mouse. So: the grab strip takes keyboard focus, and left/right arrows move it. It will say what it is and what it is for when focused, so it is usable without seeing it.

5. It stays where you put it — properly

Buzz forgets your width when you quit. I do not want to copy that. Cloud9 already keeps your theme in a small settings store on this machine (App.tsx:1550 writes it to the browser's own storage, and the settings record already carries your theme). The width — and which mode you were in — go in the same place, so both survive closing the app and are still there tomorrow morning. Buzz remembers the mode but not the width; we will remember both.

6. Double-click to put it back — with Buzz's own manners

Same as Buzz, including a detail worth copying exactly: the tooltip is conditional. At the default width it says only "Drag to resize." Once you have set a width of your own it becomes "Drag to resize. Double-click to reset width." It never offers you a reset when there is nothing to reset.

7. The take-over mode — you asked for it, and it also fixes the worst hole in this plan

You said you want both, so both get built. Copying what was measured in Buzz rather than inventing it: a control beside the Thread heading, labelled so a screen reader says "expand thread"; the thread floats out over the room; the room dims behind it rather than vanishing; one click there and one click back. And unlike Buzz, the mode and your width are both remembered after a restart.

8. The narrow window — this was a dead handle, and that is a fault, not wording

The earlier version said: when the window is too narrow for both floors, "share proportionally as today and keep your width for when there is room again". Read plainly, that is today's complaint with a handle bolted on top. Here is what it would really have done to you:

Your windowSpace beside the rail and sidebarWhat the old plan did
800 px506 px
800 − 78 rail − 216 sidebar		 Both floors need 600 px. They do not fit. Thread pinned at 232 px — below its own 300 px floor — and the handle does nothing at all when you drag it, with nothing on screen saying why.
894 px600 px The first width at which 300 + 300 both fit. Dragging starts working here.

So across the whole band from 800 px to 894 px you would have had a handle that refuses you and never says so. A refusal with no door is the thing you like least, and it would have been shipped inside the very feature meant to fix it. The fix is not better wording. It is that the band should not exist.

What I propose instead — and it costs nothing extra, because you have already asked for the mechanism. Buzz's narrow-window behaviour and Buzz's take-over mode are the same thing wearing two hats: the thread gets the whole area and there is one obvious way back. So below 894 px, Cloud9 does exactly that — the thread takes over the whole area with a back arrow to the room, which is Buzz's own answer at 800 px, and it reuses the take-over mode from point 7 rather than being a second thing to build. Above 894 px it splits and drags as normal, and your width is waiting for you when you widen the window again. Nothing on screen ever refuses you in silence.

For comparison, Buzz's own switch-over is between 800 and 850 px — it still splits and drags at 850. Ours falls at 894 because Cloud9's sidebar takes 216 px at these sizes. Same rule, different furniture.
