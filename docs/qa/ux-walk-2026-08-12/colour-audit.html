Cloud9 — why the buttons look greyed out

Colour audit of the installed app, 13 Aug 2026. Measured, not guessed.

Your report is correct, and it is not your eyes.

On this PC the main action button really is painted dark text on a dark slab — a contrast of 1.16 to 1. Anything under 4.5 to 1 is considered unreadable; 1 to 1 means the two colours are identical. The button is fully working. It just has no visible label. Every one of the 6 light palettes does this.

Honest note: the app was not clicked through

Cloud9 was already open when this started, so it was left alone rather than killed, and no second copy was opened. Instead the exact stylesheet the installed app ships was loaded into a browser engine and every palette measured directly. That gives real numbers but no screenshots and no walk of the screens.

Each cell shows the control painted in its real colours, then its contrast. Green = readable. Amber = too faint. Red = effectively invisible.

The cause, in one sentence

A palette says what colour a button is, but never says what colour the writing on it should be — so the writing is taken from whatever mode Windows is in.

  • Each of the 16 palettes sets its own backgrounds, lines and text.
  • None of them set --on-ink, --on-pine or --on-gold — the ink that sits on top of a coloured button.
  • Those three come only from a @media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) rule, i.e. from Windows, not from the palette.
  • Your Windows is in dark mode, so a light palette gets dark writing on its dark button. Black on black.

This is bad colour maths, not a disabled control. The buttons are live; users simply never click them.

Careful: the fix already on this branch makes it worse

The working copy adds a guard to that rule. Measured against the same test, it does fix the light palettes — but it raises the rule's priority so it now overrides the named dark palettes entirely. Nord, Dracula, Tokyo Night, Catppuccin, Rose Pine, Solarized dark, Graphite, Aubergine, Midnight and High Contrast Dark all collapse into one identical grey-green theme on a dark-mode PC. Ten palettes become one. It also still leaves the dark palettes broken for anyone whose Windows is in light mode (Dracula's main button measures 1.00 to 1 there).

The durable fix is for each palette to state its own on-colours, so nothing is ever borrowed from the operating system.

Other things that will read as broken

  • Box outlines are nearly invisible in 14 of 16 palettes (as low as 1.36 to 1). Text boxes, buttons and cards lose their edges, which is most of the "everything looks flat and dead" feeling.
  • Small helper text fails in 10 palettes — the sentences under settings are the ones explaining what a control does.
  • The selected room in the left rail is invisible in 5 light palettes (1.17 to 1.34 to 1), so you cannot see which room you are in.
  • "Disabled" is only ever a fade, at ten different strengths (0.32 up to 0.75) with no single rule. Two tokens meant for this, --disabled-bg and --disabled-text, are defined and never used. So a genuinely disabled button and a badly-coloured live one look the same.
  • High Contrast Light and High Contrast Dark are not high contrast where it matters — High Contrast Light's main button measures 1.14 to 1, the joint worst score in the whole app.

What was not checked

  • No screen was opened or clicked — no dead buttons, clipped layouts or stale-data checks were possible.
  • Your currently chosen palette could not be read (the app's settings store was locked). The findings hold for all 6 light palettes regardless, and the app's default, Cloud9 Pine, is one of them.
  • Hover and focus states beyond the ones listed were not measured.
